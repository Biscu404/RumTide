## 2026-10-24: Fighting Styles piece 3 (P3.B2)

The third and last piece of P3.B2, built in `engine_v3` from `docs/specs/fighting-styles.md` section 7 (D133, D140): Old Shellback reworked, and the replay told in plain words. The rework is in `combat/shellback.ts` (his shell, moods, snatch, retreat and doctrine) with small seams in `battle.ts`, `guns.ts`, `fire.ts`, `styles.ts` and `doctrine.ts`. The words are the narrative writer's (`docs/text/replay-lines.md`), kept in `combat/lines.ts` and shown by `combat/describe.ts`. GAMEPLAY is rewritten by the game designer in the same piece, and not here. Where the spec is silent the prototype on `proto-fighting-styles` was followed, and where the two disagree the spec won. **Nothing was tuned**: every number of `B.shellback` is the spec's, and the numbers of the styles and of Drill are P3.2's. Check 5 can be tuned from here (D212).

| D201 | **His shell.** Every hit on his ship does `shellback.armour` (0.8) of its damage, except in the round he rears up. It covers the great guns on every track (Round shot, Chain shot, Grapeshot, Hot Shot), a Ram, and the fire burning him, and it is a factor of the shot itself (`GunsFactors.armour`, `ramHull`, `fireLoss`), so each is rounded once. It does not touch the deck fight (his claws die to cutlasses as any crew does), and the flash of a Fire Pot is not a hit. A Mortar shell does `mortarFactor` (3) times its damage and ignores the shell, in every round, the rear up included. The old double Round shot of the rear up round is gone (`rearUpRoundShot` is removed): in that round he is simply open, so every hit lands in full. The factor comes from `shellFactor` and `shellArmour` in `shellback.ts`. The shooter aims at the enemy flagship, so in a fleet battle every ship's hit on him is shelled. A ship is Old Shellback when its captain's id is `old_shellback` (D100), so no field was added to `BattleShip` | The spec: "every hit on him does 80%, except in the round he rears up", and the Mortar's three times. D133 said the shell takes 70% of every hit (30% off); the spec, D140's numbers and the prototype say `armour` 0.8 (20% off), and the spec wins, so D133's 70% is superseded. The prototype kept `rearUpRoundShot` at 2 in its last numbers, and the brief for this piece removes it (D210) | P3.B2 |
| D202 | **His moods and the seeded rear up.** A fight with him has one roll: the round he rears up in, one of `shellback.rearUpRounds` (2, 3 or 4) through `rearUpRoundFor`, drawn once at the start of `runBattle`, only when he is in the battle, from `rngFor(seed, "battle", hunter, target, "rearUp")`. His mood in a round is closing (before it), rearing (that round) or grappling (after it), and he has none while the ships are Grappled. The battle puts the mood in his own `RoundView` (never in a captain's), and the record keeps it in his ship record (`RoundShipRecord.mood`). The round he rears up in is the battle's clock, not what his Chooser plays, so a scripted Chooser in a test gets the same open shell | The rule of D16 and D177: a roll with labels of its own. The round streams are `rngFor(seed, "battle", hunter, target, <round>)`, so a word cannot meet them. A doctrine cannot name the round (the spec), so the seam is the battle and not the doctrine | P3.B2 |
| D203 | **`Claw and Shell` is rewritten, and what became of D175.** Four rules: IF Grappled THEN Close In, Charge; IF his mood is grappling THEN Close In, Board; IF his mood is rearing THEN Hold, Hold Fire; Otherwise Close In, Round shot. The rules on rounds and the `exposed` move are gone. The D175 rule stays on top for the reason it was put there: once he holds a ship, or is held by one, the great guns are quiet and his claws Charge, so a ship in his claws never sees him rear up, and the habits are the spec's, closing in before the rear up and grappling after it. `mood` is a new condition kind that only a monster has: `validateDoctrine` refuses it in a captain's saved doctrine ("only a monster has moods"), as D102 refused `exposed`, and `DoctrineAction.exposed` and `RoundChoice.exposed` are removed. The sim's `conditionText` knows it. `upkeepBosses` puts his doctrine back at the start of every Tide, as it does his statline (D106), so the rework reaches a season already running | D102 put his habits in the doctrine system so they read like 2.5, and that stays true. A condition keeps the rule number of a round record honest, which an override in the battle would not | P3.B2 |
| D204 | **The tell, for the replay only.** In the round before he rears up his ship record carries `tell: true`, and the replay says so (`shellback.tell`). It is written only when the rear up really follows: the battle adds it to the earlier round when the next round begins as a rear up, so a fight in which the ships grapple first, or which ends first, has none. It changes nothing he plays, so no number moves. The wording is chosen by the seed and not by the round, so it does not give the round away. **No doctrine condition reads it, on purpose.** What a doctrine can read is the round after: "their last Helm move was Hold" and "their last Guns order was Hold Fire" are true once he has reared up, and then he grapples. The two ways to make the tell itself readable are both left out of this piece: a change in what he plays the round before it (which moves the balance of every style), and a new condition a captain can write ("their shell is lifting"), which would hand every doctrine the rear up round in the very round it happens, a gain the prototype never measured and check 5 would have to be searched again for. If Biscu wants a captain to time a hit to the tell, that condition is the smallest way, and P3.2 can measure it first | The spec says a captain "reads the tell in the fight". With this build that is true for a captain who watches the replay (the tell is the earliest honest sign, one round ahead) and for a doctrine that reacts to the rear up after it, which is how the prototype's best doctrines played. GAMEPLAY 2.5 says the replay shows the tell, and the writer's notes ask the same question | P3.B2 |
| D205 | **Beaten: Sunk, Captured or driven off, and never by points.** When his Hull falls below `retreatHull` (35%) of its full, or his claws below `retreatClaws` (25%), at the end of a round in which nothing was Sunk or Captured, the battle ends in that round as a Struck Colours the other side wins, and `BattleOutcome.drivenOff` says why ("hull" first, or "claws"). By D105 it is beaten (a battle with a winner), with no change to the Tide loop: `applyBattle` records the win, `recordBossWin` adds `old_shellback` to every player on the winning side, the loot is the Struck Colours share of what he carries, he is never towed, and the encounter's `result` reads "drivenOff" in place of "struckColours". The check comes after the Sunk and Captured checks and before the Escaped check. A Victory on points with him in the battle is a Draw, whichever side led (`pointsBeatHim` false), and a Draw beats nobody (D105). So five rounds with nothing decided cost the challenger nothing, and his own lead on points costs them nothing either | The spec: "beaten means Sunk, Captured, or driven off ... A Victory on points never beats him". The prototype made every points result a Draw, and the spec does not say otherwise for his own lead | P3.B2 |
| D206 | **The claw snatch.** After the Helm step, a flagship that plays Cross Their Bow while his flagship plays Close In is caught when the range after the Helm step is Short or Close: the ships are Grappled (`GrappleRecord.why` "snatch"), his side holds the edge (D162), his crew fights as a Charge with First over the rail, the other crew fights with Fists, and Lashed fast counts for his side. The guns of that round still fire at Close, the crosser's rake included, as in every grapple round. He must Close In, so a crosser in the round he rears up (he Holds) rakes him as it would any ship, and so does one who crosses from Long. Only the flagships' moves count, as with the Dash and the Ram (D91, D152): a fleet mate that crosses his bow is not caught. "At Short or Close" is read as the prototype read it, the range after the Helm step, so a ship that starts at Medium and crosses is caught | The spec and the prototype. The reading of the range and the rule for fleet mates are calls of this piece. The rake is not undone when nothing is snatched, which is why the writer's line `shellback.noRake` is left out (D211) | P3.B2 |

## 2026-10-24: The simulator learns the styles (P3.B3)

The simulator in `tools/v3/sim/` plays the six Fighting Styles on ships that really have them, with the Deck orders, Drill and Fire Pots, and runs ten checks: the five of GAMEPLAY 8.9 and five from the acceptance lists of `docs/specs/fighting-styles.md` and `docs/specs/boarding.md`. **No engine file, no number of `balance.ts` and no golden Tide changed**: it measures, and P3.2 tunes. Old Shellback is still the D175 version, so check 5 is expected to fail until piece 3 of P3.B2, and check 8 is expected to fail at the starting Drill numbers (D181). The measured results are in D197 and the table under the rows. The lines below are test lines, not gameplay numbers, so they live in the simulator (`field.ts`, `wheel.ts`, `drill.ts`, `lashed.ts`, `smuggler.ts`, `boss.ts`) and not in `balance.ts`, as D109 to D114 did.
